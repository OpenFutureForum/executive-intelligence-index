# Original research agenda — governed identities and delegated authority for AI agents

**Batch:** `BATCH-2026-013`

**Status:** Proposal only; named human review pending

**Production evidence status:** Empty

**Private target list:** None

## Executive decision

The highest-priority gap is not another definition of agent identity. It is verified evidence about **who makes which decision when an agent receives, uses, delegates, loses, or retains authority**. The repository has no production role-at-source-time evidence, no regional comparison, no implementation outcomes, and no eligible debate. Existing OFF reports already identify a directional security-and-budget tension inside selective event samples; the new agenda should test decision rights, control performance, cost, failure, and accountability without presenting OFF participants as a probability sample of executives.

The first program should be a matched-deployment research sequence: interview multiple roles about the same production agent, field a role-tagged decision-rights survey, convene a consented cross-role roundtable, and publish an implementation registry. This sequence can distinguish genuine disagreement from different responsibility and produce source records with explicit denominators, role at source time, geography, industry, rights, and limitations.

Scores in `gap-prioritization.csv` remain separate. No aggregate score is calculated. Editorial tiers identify which gaps can be addressed together through OFF’s access and formats; they do not convert subjective judgments into false precision.

## Priority sequence

1. **Decision rights and ownership.** Map who inventories, authorizes, funds, monitors, contains, decommissions, and answers to the board for one real deployment.
2. **Implementation and lifecycle outcomes.** Record architecture, identity granularity, credential lifetime, delegation, revocation latency, incident and task outcomes, cost, and abandoned designs.
3. **Oversight effectiveness.** Measure approval burden, override behavior, monitor false positives, missed events, response time, and privacy effects by action risk.
4. **Finance and assurance.** Test the budget case, loss model, investment gate, insurance, materiality, and evidence boards receive.
5. **Counterpositions and global context.** Add privacy, lawful anonymity, civil-liberties, legal, regulator, non-English, and locally grounded operator evidence.

## Existing OFF overlap and differentiation

OFF’s CISO, Executive, CEO, CFO, and CMO AI Leverage reports provide first-party, selective-community readings with bases and stated limitations. The CISO report and its Murray Newlands Show briefing already frame agent access and the absence of a dedicated governance budget. Forum Select and the CFO, CISO, CTO, CEO, public-board, and cross-suite dinner formats provide recruitment and programming fit. These materials are OFF-owned and are not independent evidence for their own claims.

This agenda must not repeat “agents have access but governance lacks budget” as a conclusion. It should add:

- role-specific and matched-organization decision evidence;
- finance and legal analysis linked to an actual control decision;
- implemented-control denominators, failures, abandoned designs, and costs;
- region- and language-specific evidence;
- credible privacy and over-control counterpositions;
- consented public-source capture that can enter the index.

No private dinner remark, attendee identity, target name, sponsor discussion, or off-the-record material is used here. Public OFF pages were checked only for overlap and format availability.

## Program architecture

### Phase 1 — instrument and consent

Approve definitions, recruit role and regional strata, separate event registration from research consent, publish the survey instrument and analysis plan, and predefine rules for attributable, anonymized, and off-the-record material. No sponsor may edit questions or see respondent-level data.

### Phase 2 — matched evidence collection

Conduct twelve interview concepts, six cross-role pairings, three surveys, and four private roundtables. Give priority to operators with a concrete deployment, incident, control decision, or abandoned design. Vendors and advisors can provide attributed perspectives but cannot replace buyer and operator evidence.

### Phase 3 — public challenge and media

Use four recorded panels to expose disagreements to challenge, then produce eight Murray briefings that explain the evidence and its limits. Public recording consent, transcript rights, exact timestamps, speaker role at source time, and correction routes are prerequisites for index ingestion.

### Phase 4 — flagship publication

Publish the State of Agent Governance Decision Rights first, followed by an implementation registry, an oversight-effectiveness report, and a global legal-and-operating atlas as sample and review thresholds are met. Each report should feed the annual State of Executive Thinking product while retaining its own methods, version, denominators, and limitations.

## Methodological safeguards

- Report OFF samples as selective and recruited, with base and denominator on every figure.
- Use “among respondents” and role-at-source-time language; never generalize to all executives.
- Pre-register intended claims, exclusions, question wording, subgroup floors, and treatment of missing data.
- Separate operator, vendor, advisor, investor, regulator, and civil-society samples.
- Preserve negative, failed, and abandoned implementations.
- Require qualified language review before translating or quoting non-English sources.
- Publish sponsor category and funding disclosure; findings, instruments, analysis, and release timing remain editorially independent.
- Provide only aggregate sponsor appendices; no participant-level or row-level access.

## Largest methodological risk

The largest risk is selection bias compounded by cross-role imbalance: OFF’s event and community access is valuable but selective, and its current role lanes, response bases, and public research are uneven. A large pooled response count would not correct that. The agenda therefore requires role-specific bases, matched organizational cases, geography and industry strata, complete denominator disclosure, and claims confined to the observed sample.
