What the source reports or argues

  • The authors report 42% pass@5 and 18% pass@1 for HPTSA with GPT-4 on the 14-vulnerability benchmark.
  • Open-source scanners and listed open-source models report 0% on this benchmark.

Important limitations

  • Only 14 selected web vulnerabilities.
  • No confidence intervals.
  • Code and prompts are withheld, limiting replication.
  • Results are capability demonstrations, not estimates of incident prevalence.

Source-located statements

6 reviewed statements are indexed from this source.

  • The HPTSA evaluation uses 14 reproducible open-source web vulnerabilities published after the tested GPT-4 knowledge cutoff. (arXiv:2406.01637v2 PDF p. 4, Section 4 and Tables 1-2)
  • On the 14-vulnerability benchmark, GPT-4-backed HPTSA reports 42% pass-at-five and 18% pass-at-one. (arXiv:2406.01637v2 PDF p. 5, Figure 2 and Section 5.2)
  • The two evaluated open-source backbones and the ZAP and Metasploit scanner baseline did not exploit any benchmark vulnerability. (arXiv:2406.01637v2 PDF pp. 1 and 5, Abstract and Figures 2-3)
  • Removing expert agents, reference documents, or the hierarchy reduced success, with the hierarchy ablation producing the largest reported decline. (arXiv:2406.01637v2 PDF p. 6, Section 5.3 and Figure 4)
  • The study demonstrates that a coordinated LLM-agent team can sometimes exploit previously unseen web vulnerabilities in a sandbox. (arXiv:2406.01637v2 PDF pp. 1 and 5, Abstract and Section 5.2)
  • The authors say the web-only sample may be biased and withhold code and prompts to reduce misuse, which limits independent replication. (arXiv:2406.01637v2 PDF pp. 8-9, Section 10 "Limitations, Ethical Considerations")
