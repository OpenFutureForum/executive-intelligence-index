The analytical model

Source, proposition, synthesis.

The framework keeps three distinct layers separate so that attribution is not confused with truth, and synthesis is not confused with universal consensus.

1

Source statements

A neutral paraphrase of what a named person or institution expressed, tied to a reproducible location in the source.

2

Scoped propositions

Compatible statements mapped to an idea and coded as supporting, challenging, qualifying or refining it.

3

Editorial synthesis

Dossiers, debates, role comparisons and briefings with explicit lineage, counterpoints and limitations.

Evidence boundaries

Designed to make uncertainty visible.

Publication safeguards

  • Use exact source locators and preserve statement lineage.
  • Record a person's role at the time of the source.
  • Separate attributed claims from independently checked facts.
  • Disclose OFF-owned evidence and source concentration.
  • Require named human approval before a research release.
  • Provide correction, rights and withdrawal paths.

What the index will not imply

  • That inclusion is endorsement or a statement is true.
  • That a selected corpus represents all executives or markets.
  • That source frequency proves prevalence, adoption or consensus.
  • That engagement measures evidence quality.
  • That private Open Future Forum conversations are public evidence.
  • That copyrighted books, transcripts or reports can be republished.
